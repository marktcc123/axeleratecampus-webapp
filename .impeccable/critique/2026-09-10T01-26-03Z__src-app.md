## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The interface is not slop; the content inside it is. The hand-drawn system is load-bearing rather than a skin — the tracker's fanned folders with die-cut status tabs, the ticket stub with punched notches and a dashed tear, the levels ladder with "YOU ARE HERE" against rung 2, the butter highlighter under "ABOUT THE MISSION". Four different objects look like four different objects, which is what the north star asks for. But the hand-drawn layer is losing an argument with the photography: all eight mission and event covers are stock JPEGs carrying **other companies' branding**, and they are the largest, brightest, first-read objects on every screen. Layout sameness has two homes: the "For you" lead tile is the same composition *and the same mission* as row one of the list 700px below, and the shop is nine byte-identical cards. The gate (`/`) is the one screen that could belong to any SaaS product, and it is the first screen anyone sees.

Absolute bans, audited: side-stripe borders **pass** (no `border-left/right ≥ 3px` in `src/app`); gradient text **pass** (only inside the logo SVG); glassmorphism **mild fail** (`ax-dialog-overlay` computes `backdrop-filter: blur(3px)`, against DESIGN.md's "nothing in this system blurs"); hero-metric templates **pass**; identical card grids **fail** (nine shop cards, five near-identical mission rows under a lead of the same composition); tiny uppercase eyebrows **drifting** (the Label caps line sits above *every* title, ~30 instances on the board alone); numbered scaffolding **pass** (the numbers are real sequences); text overflow **pass at document level** at 320 on all six screens, with one local clip ("TEA TIN SHOT" cut mid-word in Orders).

**Deterministic scan.** CLI over `src`: 6 findings, **5 false positives, 1 true**. The four `side-tab` hits are all 8–9px rotated squares with two borders — CSS-drawn chevrons, two of them carrying a source comment saying so; the `broken-image` hit matches the word `<img>` inside a JS comment, while the real tag only renders with a truthy `src`. The true positive is `application.css:225`, `.af__body { transition: max-height }`. `src/pages`: clean. Browser (`detect.js`, 8 routes): 29 finding lines over 19 element groups — `layout-transition` ×8 (all one declaration, repeated because Vite dev-mode injects the whole stylesheet graph on every route; it would not repeat in a production build), `clipped-overflow-container` ×7 (the board and shop cream panels and the drop pieces, which clip deliberately so the folder tabs bite in), `overused-font` ×5 (the body face at 31–44%, expected), `cramped-padding` ×3, `skipped-heading` ×3, `tiny-text` ×3 (Career's 10px `.crr__card-note`). Zero page errors and zero failed requests on all 8 routes.

Where they agree: the detector's `skipped-heading` hits are the machine's view of the heading-order problem the review found by hand, and its `tiny-text` hits land on Career's note line. Where the detector was wrong: five of its six CLI findings. Where it caught what the review missed: the `cramped-padding` on the lead tile's card.

**Visual overlays.** Browser automation here is headless Playwright, so **no user-visible overlay exists**; the browser findings are console output captured programmatically. Injection preflight and all 8 injections succeeded; the live server started on port 8400 and was stopped cleanly, with `index.html` byte-identical before and after.

## Overall Impression

The measured floor is genuinely clean — zero contrast failures across 13 screens, zero targets under 44px, a visible focus ring on every one of 14 consecutive tab stops, no horizontal overflow at 320. That is the part most teams skip. What holds the product back is not craft, it is content and lead: every mission is illustrated by a stranger's marketing poster, the board's answer to "what should I do today" is repeated as list item one, and the seat flow denies and performs the same action in a single bar. The biggest single win available is nine drawn covers.

## What's Working

1. **The accessibility floor, measured rather than asserted.** 13 screens, zero text/background contrast failures (the only hits were `sr-only` at 1×1px with `clip-path: inset(50%)`); zero interactive elements under 44px in either dimension; a 2px focus ring on every tab stop with focus scrolled into view; `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` renders the board complete rather than blank.
2. **The withdraw sheet as a model of money honesty.** Amount with "$133 AVAILABLE", destination, speed with its fee priced inline, then Withdraw / Fee / **You receive** restated above the button. It never makes you compute anything.
3. **"A locked thing states its distance", executed everywhere.** Locked rows read "LV.4 · 2,620 XP AWAY"; every locked rung on Levels carries the gap *and* its translation into missions; the board's empty state names the filters you switched on and hands you the way out.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Every mission and event cover is a stock poster for someone else's event.** All 8 files in `src/assets/covers/poster-*.jpg` are third-party marketing graphics with legible foreign branding, mapped onto unrelated missions: the Founder AMA opens with an IDX Channel ESG conference poster reading "JOIN US! ON JULY" above a Sep 8 event — and the same poster is the face of the ticket the student holds up at the door; a São Paulo D2C Summit boarding pass illustrates a K-beauty unboxing reel on the lead tile, in the list, on the detail page and on the public profile. *Why it matters:* it contradicts the north star's first sentence, it undoes the hand-drawn identity by putting the loudest objects on screen in a different visual language, and it puts a stranger's logo on the one artifact meant to be shown to a human being. *Fix:* five mission covers and four event covers, drawn in the system's own hand — the five SVG packshots added to the shop today prove the pipeline works. Until they exist, `ImageSlot`'s dashed sketch slot reads as "not filled yet" rather than as a lie. *Command:* `/impeccable craft mission covers`.

**[P1] The seat flow denies and performs the same action, and the ticket has no date on its face.** After submitting the seat sheet, the bar reads "Nothing was sent. Seats open at launch." while the count drops 8 → 7 and the CTA becomes "See pass »" that issues a real ticket with your name on it. Separately, the stub's caps line is built from `weekday: 'short'` + `month: 'short'` with **no day number** — the ticket reads "TUE · SEP · STUDENT UNION, ROOM 2B" — and "Doors at 7pm." is hardcoded directly under "TIME 6–7pm". *Verified:* all three still true after the harden pass. *Already fixed by that pass:* the pass is addressed by event id (two board events collided on date and resolved to the fixture's ticket under another name), an event the student already holds a pass to no longer offers a seat, the note the sheet takes is shown on the pass, and the inputs are bounded and wrap. *Fix:* pick one truth — either don't decrement and don't issue a pass, or drop "Nothing was sent" and say what the pass is; add `day: 'numeric'`; derive the doors line from the event's own start time or delete it. *Command:* `/impeccable clarify` for the copy, a small edit for the date.

**[P1] My orders shows production notes where the photos should be, and names the wrong brand.** Two of three rows render `ImageSlot`'s dashed placeholder with its internal label showing — "JOURNAL SHOT" and "TEA TIN SHOT", the second clipped mid-word — beside one row with a real photograph, although both products now have art in the shop. And `hub.example.json` gives ord1 `"brand": "Intelligent Change"` while the same product `p1` is `"brand": "Notely"` in the shop. *Verified both.* The brand mismatch was introduced today when p1 was reassigned to Notely so the brand pages had products; Orders duplicates the brand instead of reading it. *Fix:* resolve the order's cover through `productId` (the join already exists) and read the brand off the product. *Command:* `/impeccable harden src/app/screens/Orders.jsx`.

**[P2] Three heading defects, two of which a screen reader hits immediately.** `/app/earn` has **no `h1` at all** — the home screen begins at H2 "For you". `/app/perks` puts H2 "Weekly drop" *before* H1 "Perks shop". `/app/me/career` announces "**DDermabell**" and "**TTech & gaming gear**" because the brand Avatar's initial sits inside the heading without `aria-hidden`. *All three verified in the DOM.* The detector's three `skipped-heading` hits are the same family. *Fix:* an `h1` on the board (the tab's own name, visually hidden if the design does not want it drawn); swap the shop's heading levels; `aria-hidden` on the disc. *Command:* `/impeccable audit src/app`.

**[P2] The board's top third is two hidden scrollers and a duplicate.** The "For you" tile's mission is *also* the first Missions row 700px below — same title, same art; `pickToday` has no exclusion. Between them, an Events grid 1,185px wide in a 390px viewport (8 events, ~2.2 visible) and a Brands row 542px wide (7 brands, ~5 visible), **both with `scrollbar-width: none`**, no dots, no "See all", brand names truncated to "Axelerate …". Keyboard: 18 tab stops before the first mission, no skip link. *Why it matters:* repeating the lead as row one turns "we picked this for you" back into "item 1 of a list" — the job-board reading the anti-references forbid — and 15 of 21 items on the screen are invisible with nothing saying so. *Fix:* filter the lead out of the list (one predicate); cap Events at 3 with a "See all events »" row or give the scroller a position cue; add a skip link. *Command:* `/impeccable layout src/app/screens/GigsBoard.jsx`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (one-handed, distracted).** Two hidden horizontal scrollers stacked in the top half of the board, scrollbars suppressed: a vertical flick starting inside either will stall or slide sideways with no indication. The Brands row is worse — the disc she is aiming at is labelled "Axelerate …", so the target has no name. In the shop, after adding an item, two bag glyphs sit 60px apart (the Perks tab's active blob, the cart FAB with its "1") meaning "you are here" and "your cart".

**Jordan (first-timer).** Three unlabelled header glyphs: a flame (quests), a bell (inbox), an arrow (share). The quests dialog then offers "+1 freeze" beside five XP rewards with nothing saying what a freeze is. On the mission page, three initial discs "J N S" sit beside "SPOTS 4/12" unlabelled. He taps the "Notely" disc and lands on a page with one mission, one product and blank space below. He taps Apply, the biggest black button in the app, and gets "Nothing was sent."

**Sam (screen reader / keyboard / 4.5:1).** No `h1` on the board; inverted heading order in the shop; "DDermabell" announced on Career; 18 tab stops to the first mission with no skip link; input placeholders at 2.44:1 and 2.61:1 against PRODUCT.md's own 4.5:1 rule (a design-system fix, Principle 5). What works and should be said: a visible 2px focus ring on all 14 consecutive tab stops with focus scrolled into view, no target under 44px anywhere, `aria-live` on checkout errors, `role="status"` on both result counts, and zero contrast failures across the 13 base screens.

**The 90-second student between classes.** The lead tile genuinely answers the question — one mission, its length, its deadline, its pay, above the fold. That is the best thing today's work added. Then it undercuts itself three ways: the largest object on the tile is a São Paulo boarding pass and the smallest is the "$25"; the same mission is row one 700px down, so the pick reads as list position rather than a choice made for her; and between the answer and the list sit 15 items she cannot see.

## Minor Observations

- `GigsBoard.jsx` pins `NEW_SLUG = 'fable-coffee-morning-routine'`, which matches **no** slug in the fixture — the "new" badge can never render. *Verified.*
- Career repeats "Brand track certificate / Partner-specific internship proof for this brand." verbatim under both brand lanes; the mission's benefit bullets pick icons at random (`coffee-cup-2` for "keep the pack", `star` for "credit lands fast", while `star` also means "K-beauty" in the tag row on the same screen).
- **Lacquer is carrying information it cannot carry.** Its slashed zero reads as an 8: "5,620 XP to Syndicate" misread as 5,628, "820 to Insider" as 82.8 — exactly the numbers Principle 2 mandates, in the one face the One Note Rule says should never carry information.
- The "Added to your cart" toast survives navigation into `/app/cart` and sits under the cart it is announcing.
- The four guest cards on the event page carry each name three times (Lacquer, body, "Verified student") — four Lacquer notes in one view against the One Note Rule.
- Career encodes the same fact three times per lane: dots, "2/3 finished", "1 more gig to unlock". Its dots are evenly spaced solid circles, the one place in the student app where Principle 4's hand-drawn data reads as a stock progress stepper.
- `$14.09` and `$27.22` sit beside $32 / $39 / $24 / $45 / $38 — two scraped-looking prices in a fixture of round numbers.
- The Wallet band is orange and Career's is pink; PRODUCT.md Principle 7 names five bands and lists career as cyan. Both new bands measure clean, but the record has diverged from the app.
- My Events renders "crew" and "going" as pills and "DONE" as bare grey caps.
- Checkout step 1's "Pay with card" says "Checks out in the shop — card, receipt and tracking there", then still collects a full address in-app. Worth confirming the address is not collected twice.

## Questions to Consider

1. The lead tile exists to answer "is there anything worth my time today?" So why is the largest, brightest object on it a stock photo of a São Paulo boarding pass, and the smallest thing on it the $25?
2. Five hand-drawn packshots landed in the shop today and they are the best-looking things in the app. Would you rather ship five hand-drawn *mission* covers and zero photographs than nine covers of which eight are someone else's marketing?
3. The board opens with four headings of identical weight. If a student reads only one section before the lecture starts, which one is it — and does the page say so anywhere?
4. Principle 2 says a locked thing states its distance, and the app states that distance in a face where 820 reads as 82.8. Does the rule survive its own typeface?
5. Brand pages were built because a brand disc needed somewhere to lead. With one mission and one product on them, and a page that does not scroll — is the disc row the feature and the page the tax?
