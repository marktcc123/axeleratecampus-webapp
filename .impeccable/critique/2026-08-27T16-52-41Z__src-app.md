## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Not AI-generated, and not close.** Sixteen screens hold one specific
vocabulary: hand-drawn blobs behind the current tab, folder tabs that bite into
the panel below them, a butter scribble under an active filter, tally strokes,
a taped sheet on the mission detail, DynaPuff screen titles, applications as a
pile of coloured folders you leaf down into. None of that is a default. There
are no eyebrows-per-section in the AI sense (the caps labels are the design
system's own `--font-label` role, used as a system), no gradient text, no
glassmorphism, no hero-metric block, no identical card grid — the locked tile
and the sold-out card differ structurally, not just in colour.

Against the product-register test — would someone fluent in Linear or Stripe
trust this — mostly yes. The two things that would give them pause are the same
two the score above names: type that does not sit on a scale, and a nav bar
whose destinations you have to learn by tapping.

**Deterministic scan** (`detect.mjs`, 4 findings):
- `application.css:143` — `transition: max-height` on the folder brief. Real,
  though it is the standard accordion technique; `grid-template-rows` is the
  modern replacement.
- `sub-screen.css:18`, `gigs-detail.css:112`, `me-hub.css:155` — flagged as
  side-stripe borders. **All three are false positives**: each is a CSS chevron,
  two borders on a square rotated 45 or 135 degrees.

**Visual overlay**: not presented. That flow needs a browser window shown to
the user and this environment runs headless. Evidence came from headless
measurement instead: a sixteen-route tour, heading outlines, content-fill
ratios, and the type-size distribution.

## What's Working

1. **The copy is the best thing in the product.** It converts abstractions into
   the unit a student actually thinks in — XP into missions, credit into shop
   dollars, a closed door into a distance. "This mission has left the board" is
   a better answer than a dead link, and somebody had to decide that.
2. **The system reads as one app across sixteen screens.** The folder tab, the
   cream panel, the caps label, the handwritten aside and the blob all recur
   with the same meaning. Sub-screens share one shell, so a back row is always
   in the same place.
3. **Honesty is built in, not bolted on.** The clipboard does not claim a copy
   it did not make. The countdown counts to a real Friday. A sold-out card has
   no button rather than a disabled one. Credit never appears without what it
   is worth.

## Priority Issues

**[was P1 — fixed 2026-08-27, `c8ffdc2` + DS `e36e95a`] The app's type is not on the design system's scale**
- Measured wider than this snapshot did: **130** hardcoded px in `src/app` across
  **25** distinct values (9px to 27px, with half-pixel steps at 1.03 ratios), plus
  11 more in the legacy files. 54 sat below `--text-xs`.
- Root cause was in the system, not the app: there was no rung below 12px, so six
  different sizes invented one; and the scale was px, so it could not be reached
  by a reader's own font-size setting.
- Sorted by selector, weight and family, the 25 values were expressing about seven
  roles. Each declaration was mapped by role rather than nearest value onto
  `3xs·xs·sm·lg·xl` = 10·12·15·18·22 at ratios 1.20–1.25. Body prose (185-character
  passages in `.gd__prose`) went 13.5px to 15px. Two hero elements reach `2xl`.
- Also found and fixed while routing h1s through the scale: `responsive.css` stepped
  `--text-2xl` down to 20px on phones but left `--text-xl` at 22px, so below 641px
  the scale ran backwards at exactly the sizes screen titles use.
- Guards: `tests/unit/type-scale.test.js` (no hardcoded size; no undefined rung; the
  shell stays on the five rungs), a ladder-direction assertion in the responsive unit
  test, and an e2e test that resolves rungs through a real element. All three verified
  to fail on a reintroduced violation. Verified: type scales correctly at a 20px and
  24px reader root with no horizontal scroll.

**[P1] The board has no h1, and the gate has no headings at all**
- `/gigs` is the app's home screen and its outline starts at h2 ("Active
  quests"). `/` has zero headings. `/gigs/:slug`, `/me/levels` and `/me/orders`
  jump h1 to h3.
- Why: a screen with no h1 has no landmark to jump to, and a skipped level
  tells a screen-reader user a section is missing. The fix is free — the
  headings exist, they are at the wrong levels.
- Fix: give the board an h1, demote the tile titles to h3 under an h2, and
  close the h2 gaps on the three screens that skip.
- Command: `/impeccable audit`

**[P2] Invite and Career stop two-thirds up the screen**
- Measured fill against usable height: Invite 63%, Career 62%. Co-creations
  77% and Syndicate 73% sit just behind them.
- Why: on a phone a screen that ends early reads as unfinished rather than
  spacious, and both screens end without a closing action — Invite tells you
  how the referral works and then offers nothing but Copy code.
- Fix: give each a terminal action (share the code, send your profile) and let
  the content breathe into the space rather than stopping short of it.
- Command: `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] The invite tally reads as three bare strokes**
- `TallyCount` renders `|||` with "3 of 5 codes used" beneath. The two unused
  codes are not drawn, so the mark carries the count and not the proportion.
- Why: the design system's own rule is that a figure with no honest proportion
  just stands as type. Three strokes floating above a sentence is the worst of
  both — it looks like a rendering fault.
- Fix: draw all five and strike the used ones, or drop the mark and let the
  sentence do the work.
- Command: `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] The tab bar is icon-only for sighted users**
- Labels are in the DOM and clipped away, so assistive tech has them and eyes
  do not. A flag, a bag, a checklist and a person are not guessable on a first
  run, and the app's own first critique flagged exactly this.
- Why: it is the recognition cost a tab bar exists to remove. The blob colour
  does help orientation, but only after the mapping is learned.
- Fix: this is the owner's call, made twice, to match the design. Worth
  revisiting once real students try it; a first-run hint would cost little.
- Command: `/impeccable onboard`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer)**: four unlabelled icons at the bottom. Nothing on any
screen says what XP is for, what a level opens before you find `/me/levels`, or
what "K-beauty" filters. Taps the bag on a shop card and nothing visible
happens — no cart, no confirmation, no count.

**Sam (accessibility)**: the home screen has no h1 and the gate has no headings
at all. Three screens skip a heading level. Format tabs carry `role="tab"`
without roving tabindex, so all four take their own tab stop. White on brand
pink is 4.31:1 on the quest card and the application folders, a known and
recorded exception rather than an oversight.

**Casey (distracted mobile)**: type down to 9px on a device held at arm's
length. Every filter is component state, so backgrounding the app and coming
back resets the board with no warning. The thumb zone holds the tab bar and
little else — the board's own controls all sit in the top half.

## Minor Observations

- `transition: max-height` on the application folder animates a layout
  property. `grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr` does the same job without it.
- The shop's bag button has no visible result. It is the only control in the
  app that appears to do nothing.
- The mission detail's "The mission" heading sits at h3 under an h1, and the
  accordion headings below it are buttons rather than headings, so the screen's
  outline is thinner than its content.
- Screens carry between 40 and 232 words. The short ones are not obviously
  worse, but Settings at 40 words and Levels at 232 use the same shell, and the
  shell suits the long one better.
